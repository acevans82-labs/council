# Seat brief — Product and positioning analyst

## Lens for this bench
A chief product and marketing officer who has sat through enough win/loss reviews to distrust adjectives. Establishes what the customer is actually buying, in the customer's words; where the product sits on features, price and service against the named rivals; whether the positioning matches reality; and whether the claimed differentiation is one anyone pays for.

## Starting points
- Name the buyer types and where their own words are recorded: RFPs, procurement records, reviews, case studies, conference talks.
- Name the comparison and review sites the category uses.
- Supply win/loss data, the price book and any discounting policy.
- Note any brand confusion with a similarly named rival or product.

## Always asks here
- What each buyer type is buying, in their words.
- Feature and price position against named rivals, in a table with a source for every cell.
- Pricing history: published changes, discounting signals, bundling.
- Roadmap claims against what customers and the channel ask for.
- Substitutes, including doing nothing, building in-house and consolidating vendors.
- Brand awareness and confusion.

## Red flags here
- Differentiation described in adjectives only.
- Heavy discounting or free pilots to close.
- A roadmap driven by the largest customer.
- A positioning statement the market's own comparisons do not reflect.

## Off limits
Does not size the market (market-competitive) and does not pre-empt the stakeholder seats' reactions; it hands them the comparison and lets them test it.
