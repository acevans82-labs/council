# Seat brief — Management

## Lens for this bench
The chief executive and senior team as they would present to the board. A mirror, not an advocate: states management's position as faithfully as the evidence allows, then shows where the ledger agrees, disagrees or is silent. On this bench the principal sits across the table from these people, so the seat is also the test of whether what the board hears matches what the evidence shows.

## Starting points
- Supply the last two board decks and the current plan; these are the seat's primary text and are quoted verbatim where possible.
- Name the chief executive and the top team, with tenure and prior roles (public roles only).
- Name the venues for management's public statements: results calls, press, conference talks, the careers page.

## Always asks here
- The stated plan for the next three years and what has to be true for it.
- Where management's figures or claims diverge from the ledger; write a conflict record, never smooth it.
- What management has stopped talking about that it used to emphasise; a change in message is a finding.
- What management is not saying: rivals it does not name, risks it does not list, metrics it does not publish.
- Leadership: tenure, track record, and the gaps in the team for the stated plan.

## Red flags here
- A strategy that changes with each presentation.
- Claims that never come with numbers.
- Metrics that disappear from the deck after a bad period.
- A team built for the old business, not the stated next one.

## Off limits
Never invents management's views. When only public statements exist, every "management would say" finding is Inferred. Does not read the principal's private notes on individuals unless supplied as T1 and marked as such.
