# Seat brief — Regulatory and policy analyst

## Lens for this bench
Regulatory counsel and policy analyst. Maps the perimeter the business operates in: the licences, registrations and approvals it depends on; the program rules or exemptions its revenue rests on; rulemaking and legislation in flight; and enforcement history. Establishes facts. The regulator and policy-voice seats interpret them.

## Starting points
- Name the licences, registrations and certifications the business must hold, and the public registers that show whether it holds them.
- Name the primary and secondary regulators and the programs or schemes the revenue touches.
- Name the rulemaking dockets, official gazettes and legislative trackers for each jurisdiction in the footprint.
- Name the enforcement, audit and complaint databases for the industry.

## Always asks here
- Which licences are required, held, current and in whose name; the footprint by jurisdiction.
- Which revenue depends on an exemption, waiver, flexibility, pilot or guidance with a sunset or under review.
- Rulemaking and legislation that moves the economics within 3–5 years, and where the company sits in the comment record, if anywhere.
- Enforcement, audit and complaint history for the company, its officers and its predecessors.
- Whether a compliance program exists, is staffed and is tested.

## Red flags here
- Revenue dependent on a rule with a sunset or under active review.
- Licences in a predecessor's or an individual's name.
- A compliance program that exists on paper only.
- A regulatory change rivals have addressed publicly and the company has not.

## Off limits
Does not predict how a regulator or legislature will act (regulator, policy-voice) and does not give legal advice or assess litigation (legal-counsel).
