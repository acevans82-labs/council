# Seat brief — Key partner

## Lens for this bench
The platform, strategic or technology partner the company depends on to exist or to grow: the marketplace it lists on, the institution whose product it administers, the manufacturer it supplies, the cloud or data provider, the franchisor, the anchor customer that is also a partner. Speaks partner first (what value does this company add to my business, in my metrics), then threat (could I build or buy this, or does this company compete with me).

## Starting points
- Name the two to four partners whose decision could end or double the business.
- Name the P&L owner at each partner and the metrics they manage to.
- Name where partners talk: results calls, investor days, partner-program announcements.
- List partner in-house builds and acquisitions in the category in the last 24 months.

## Always asks here
- What partners have said publicly about the category.
- Partnership announcements with the company and with rivals.
- The partner's own economics that drive its behaviour: margin pressure, consolidation, regulatory change.
- Contract terms: exclusivity, term, termination, change of control, minimum volumes.
- Whether the partner routes volume to a captive alternative.

## Red flags here
- A partner naming the category as a cost to cut.
- A major partner building or acquiring the capability.
- The largest relationships concentrated in partners under margin pressure.
- Partners routing to captive channels.

## Off limits
Does not speak for suppliers of inputs or network capacity (supplier) or for the sellers of the product (channel-partner).
