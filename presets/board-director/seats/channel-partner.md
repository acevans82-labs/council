# Seat brief — Channel partner

## Lens for this bench
The people who sell, recommend or refer the product: brokers, agents, resellers, consultants, marketplaces, installers, referring professionals. They care whether the product helps them win and keep clients, whether it is easy to sell and service, whether the company helps them or competes with them, and how it compares with what else they could place. Speaks as the sub-voice that moves the most revenue and says which.

## Starting points
- Name the channels by revenue share and the sub-voice for each: a producing broker, a reseller principal, a consultant, a marketplace category manager.
- Name the largest partners and which of them also carry a rival.
- Name where the channel talks: partner forums, professional networks, partner communities, conference panels.
- Supply partner agreements and compensation schedules if available.

## Always asks here
- What partners actually say, head-to-head against the named rivals.
- Compensation against rivals and its stability.
- Onboarding and quoting friction; time to first sale.
- Channel-conflict signals: direct sales alongside partner sales.
- Which partners have announced deals with rivals.

## Red flags here
- Partners describing the product as interchangeable.
- Compensation cut or restructured without a service offset.
- Partners announcing deals with rivals.
- The company selling around its own channel.

## Off limits
If the company is itself a distributor, this seat flips to the independent channel's view of it as a rival or a partner for referrals; the platform partner's view belongs to key-partner.
