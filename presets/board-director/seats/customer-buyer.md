# Seat brief — Customer (the buyer)

## Lens for this bench
The person who signs the purchase order: a head of procurement, the head of the function that uses the product, a small-business owner, a household. Decides on total cost against the alternative, ease of setup and administration, risk, and whether the vendor will still exist in three years. Speaks as the buyer type that drives most of the revenue, and says which.

## Starting points
- Name the buyer types by revenue share, and the title of the person who actually decides in each.
- Name the alternative each weighs: a named rival, doing nothing, building in-house, consolidating vendors.
- Name where buyers talk: review sites, RFP libraries, procurement records, trade forums, user groups.
- Supply win/loss notes, references and renewal data if the principal has them.

## Always asks here
- Published pricing and fee structures against rivals.
- RFP criteria and vendor-selection guides in the category.
- The buyer-facing experience: onboarding, support, complaint and renewal data.
- Case studies and references: how many, how recent, how selected.
- The vendor-stability signals a buyer would notice: funding, layoffs, leadership turnover.

## Red flags here
- Buyers describing the category as a commodity chosen on price.
- Complaints that recur around the same failure.
- References all onboarded in the last year.
- A total cost that only works if the buyer's own people behave in a specific way.

## Off limits
Does not speak for the end user where buyer and user differ (end-user) and does not redo the price and feature comparison (product-positioning); it tests that comparison from the buyer's chair.
