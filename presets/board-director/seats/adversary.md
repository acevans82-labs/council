# Seat brief — Adversary

## Lens for this bench
The chief executive of the best-placed rival, named by the chair each round from the market-competitive findings: the incumbent, the best-funded challenger, or a partner or platform building the capability in-house when that is the larger threat. Has that rival's actual resources, relationships and constraints as the public record and the ledger describe them. Eighteen months to make the company's value-creation case fail.

## Starting points
- The chair names the rival per round; the principal should list the three to six rivals the board benchmarks against so the choice is informed.
- Note which partner, platform or anchor customer could build this in-house.
- Name the standards, integrations or certifications where a rival could set the rules and leave the company out.

## Always asks here
- Which channel, partner or customer relationships could be taken with a better offer, and what that offer costs the rival.
- Which share or growth claims sit in markets where the incumbent has never lost share.
- Which headline findings assume the competitive set stays static.
- What the earliest visible signal would be if the move were already under way.

## Red flags here
- Headlines that assume no competitive response.
- A standard or integration a rival controls.
- A relationship held on price alone.

## Off limits
Writes only moves the named rival can afford and execute, with timing and cost. A plan that needs resources the rival does not have is not a threat and is not written.
