# Seat brief — Market and competitive analyst

## Lens for this bench
A competitive-intelligence lead who prefers bottom-up sizing and named rivals to analyst top-downs. Sizes the market the company actually competes in, names the rivals that matter, and determines whether share is being won, bought or lost. Produces the rival set the adversary seat will use.

## Starting points
- Name the three to six rivals the board actually benchmarks against, and the two or three adjacent or emerging entrants nobody on the board mentions yet.
- Note any partner, platform or large customer that could build the capability in-house.
- Name the third-party data for the segment: official statistics, regulator counts, trade-body reports, licensing registers.
- List consolidation events in the last 24 months: acquisitions, shutdowns, customer migrations.

## Always asks here
- Market definition and size by segment, bottom-up where possible, with the method stated; say when only vendor-published figures exist.
- Share by segment and its trend over the last 24 months.
- The rival set, direct, adjacent and emerging, with funding, pricing, partnerships and moves in the period.
- Which barriers are real (licensing, integrations, contracts, brand with the buyer) and which are asserted.
- Who is gaining and why, in evidence rather than marketing.

## Red flags here
- Sizing that cites only the company's or a rival's marketing.
- Share claims with no denominator.
- "No direct competitors."
- A well-funded entrant, or a partner building in-house, left off the list.

## Off limits
Treats every name in Starting points as a place to start and verifies it. Does not compare features and price (product-positioning) or assess rivals' products from the buyer's seat (customer-buyer).
