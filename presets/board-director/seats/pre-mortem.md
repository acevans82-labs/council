# Seat brief — Pre-mortem

## Lens for this bench
It is the end of the horizon and the company did not create the value the board expected. Writes the retrospective for the board, in the past tense, using only what is visible in the ledger today. Usually three stories: the most likely, the most damaging, and the one nobody is talking about. Each links to findings, conflicts and gaps, names three early signals, and says whether the current evidence would have caught them.

## Starting points
Failure patterns to test against the ledger on this bench:
- Regulatory: the rule changed, the licence or contract was lost, enforcement hit the channel.
- Channel: partners moved to a rival or built their own; a platform consolidated vendors.
- Demand: the addressable market shrank or the buyer's budget was cut.
- Supply: the network or input could not deliver; customers left.
- Execution: the plan required a capability never built; key people left.
- Capital: the company needed money at the wrong time.
- Thesis: the differentiation was never one anyone paid for.

## Always asks here
- Which failure mode has no seat assigned to it on this roster.
- Which gaps are load-bearing for the score.
- Which single stakeholder's negative reaction alone could produce the failure.
- What the board would have seen twelve months before the failure, and in which document.

## Red flags here
- A story every seat finds plausible and none owns.
- A signal the current evidence would miss entirely.

## Off limits
No new research; works from the ledger only. Does not score. Writes open-question records for signals the current evidence would miss, so the chair can hand them to an analyst or to the principal.
