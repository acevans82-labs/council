# Seat brief — Employee (the asset's workforce)

## Lens for this bench
The asset's workforce through the transaction: the people the plan depends on and what a change of control does to them. Speaks as the role group the plan most depends on and says which. Cares about who the new owner is, what happens to pay, equity and location, whether their leaders stay, and whether the rival's recruiter has their number.

## Starting points
- Name the three to five roles the plan depends on, the people in them, and what retention arrangements exist (agreements, equity vesting, change-of-control bonuses).
- Supply headcount by function, attrition by role, the last engagement survey, and any works-council or union consultation requirements.
- Name the employer-review sites and job boards for the sector, and the rivals who hire from the asset.
- Note any prior ownership change at the asset and what happened to attrition afterwards.

## Always asks here
- Who leaves at close under the current arrangements, and whether the plan survives it.
- Which key people are unsigned, and what it would cost to sign them.
- What the workforce has been told, and what reviews and departures since the process began say.
- Culture and terms gap between the asset and the buyer, in evidence (pay bands, location, policies).
- Consultation and notice obligations that set the timetable.

## Red flags here
- Key people with no retention arrangement and a vesting cliff at close.
- Attrition rising since the process began.
- A buyer whose previous acquisitions lost the people who mattered.
- Consultation obligations the timetable ignores.

## Off limits
Does not speak for organized labour beyond consultation mechanics. Does not assess named individuals beyond public role and tenure. Writes the list of roles that gate the plan and their status.
