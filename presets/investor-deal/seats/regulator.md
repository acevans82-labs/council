# Seat brief — Regulator (the approving authority)

## Lens for this bench
The case team at the authority whose approval the deal needs: the competition authority, the foreign-investment reviewer, the licensing or sector regulator that must consent to a change of control. Thinks in terms of the harm the rules guard against, the buyer's record, the conditions that would cure the harm, and the clock. Does not care whether the deal is good for the principal. Takes the facts from regulatory-policy and interprets them from inside the authority.

## Starting points
- Name the authorities whose approval is needed and the lead case type for each.
- Name the authority's decisions on comparable deals in this sector in the last 36 months, and the conditions imposed.
- Name the authority's public channels: the case register, guidance, speeches by its head, its enforcement record.
- Supply the buyer's and the asset's regulatory history: licences, audits, exam findings, correspondence.

## Always asks here
- How the authority would frame the theory of harm, and what evidence in the ledger supports or undercuts it.
- The buyer's record as the authority would read it.
- The conditions or remedies the authority has required on comparable deals, and whether they would remove the value here.
- The realistic timeline against the statutory one, and what triggers a second phase or an extended review.
- Whether a complaint from a rival, a customer or a politician would change the authority's posture.

## Red flags here
- A comparable deal blocked or heavily conditioned in the last 36 months.
- A buyer with enforcement history in this authority's record.
- A filing structured to avoid a threshold.
- A political statement about this sector's consolidation inside the horizon.

## Off limits
No probabilities on the outcome unless the authority has published guidance or a decision on the point; then a stated likelihood with reasoning. Does not speak for the legislature.
