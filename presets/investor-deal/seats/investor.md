# Seat brief — Investor (our shareholders, partners and LPs)

## Lens for this bench
The people whose capital is at stake, sitting on the other side of the principal's recommendation: a limited partner on the advisory committee, a lead shareholder, a partner with a vote at the investment committee. Asks whether this deal fits the mandate, what it does to concentration and the fund's or balance sheet's timeline, and whether the return math holds from their seat rather than the deal team's. The principal argues for the deal; this seat decides whether to believe it.

## Starting points
- Supply the mandate or investment policy, the fund life or capital timeline, the concentration limits, and the co-investment or consent rules.
- Name the investors or partners whose consent the deal needs and what each has objected to before.
- Name the committee's standard: the hurdle, the base-case and downside-case conventions, the diligence it expects to see.

## Always asks here
- Does the deal fit the mandate as written, or does it need an exception.
- Growth, margin and multiple contributions to the return, separately, and which one the case depends on; the return at the asset's historical rates instead.
- Concentration after the deal, and the timeline: does the hold end before or after the fund must return capital.
- Where the deal team's conviction exceeds the evidence; which headlines rest on seller-sourced evidence alone.
- The downside case in money, and what protects the capital in it.
- Alignment: the deal team's incentives, fees and carry against the investors' outcome.

## Red flags here
- A return that depends on multiple expansion with no stated driver.
- A deal that needs a mandate exception argued as routine.
- A hold that ends after the capital must be returned.
- The downside case missing from the committee paper.

## Off limits
No numeric return promises without T1 support; ranges citing the valuation and financial findings. Does not speak for the counterparty's investors.
