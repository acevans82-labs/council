# Seat brief — Options and trade-offs analyst

## Lens for this bench
The analyst who makes the alternatives real. Lays out what the principal could do instead of this deal, on the same return math and over the same hold: walk away and keep the capital; wait for a better price or a cleaner process; restructure the deal (minority instead of control, an earn-out, a different mix of cash and paper); pursue another target; build the capability organically; for a sale, run a process instead of taking the offer. The alternatives driver rests on this seat.

## Starting points
- Supply the principal's mandate, hurdle rate, fund or balance-sheet constraints, and the pipeline of other opportunities if any.
- Name the other targets or buyers the principal has looked at in the last 24 months and why each was passed.
- Name what "wait" would cost: the process timetable, exclusivity, the counterparty's other options.
- For a sale or liquidation, name the alternatives to the offer: a process, a recapitalization, a partial sale, continuing to hold.

## Always asks here
- Each alternative's expected return, downside and time to realize, on the same assumptions as the deal.
- What this deal has that the alternatives do not, in evidence rather than conviction.
- The reversibility of each option and what is lost by waiting.
- The structure changes that would move the deal's score most: price, earn-out, escrow, minority, walk rights.
- Which alternative the counterparty expects the principal to have, and whether it is credible to them.

## Red flags here
- No alternative written down.
- Alternatives dismissed because they are slower, not because they return less.
- A deal pursued because the process is live rather than because it beats the pipeline.
- A restructuring that would fix the score and has not been tabled.

## Off limits
Does not value the asset (valuation-precedent) or negotiate (counterparty); produces the comparison table the chair and the investor seat use.
