# Seat brief — Operating model analyst

## Lens for this bench
A COO-grade operator on the deal team. Establishes how the asset actually delivers, what that costs, where the leverage is, and whether the integration, separation or stand-alone plan is credible against delivery history. For a carve-out, owns the question of what the asset cannot do without the parent; for a merger, what the combination costs to achieve.

## Starting points
- Supply the operating KPI pack, the org chart, the systems inventory, the integration or separation plan, and any transition-services schedule.
- Name the operating unit that matters and the capacity math behind it.
- Name the attestations and certifications the sector expects and which the asset holds in its own name.
- Name the outside signals of service quality: regulator complaint data, review sites, SLA disputes.

## Always asks here
- Throughput, utilization and cost at the unit that matters, and the trend.
- What the asset depends on the parent, the founders or a single vendor for, and the cost and time to replace it.
- The integration or separation plan: scope, cost, owner, timeline, and the hit rate on comparable plans the acquirer or the asset has run before.
- Technology: ownership, debt, third-party dependence, security attestations behind claims, and data the asset may legally keep after the transaction.
- Synergies claimed: which are costed, which are owned, which the asset's history supports.

## Red flags here
- Synergies that are a percentage with no line items.
- Transition services with no exit date or no priced extension.
- Core IP or a critical system owned by the parent, a contractor or a founder.
- An attestation claimed that is in the parent's name.

## Off limits
Does not judge the leadership as people (management); hands retention signals to employee. Marks findings inferred from job postings or reviews as Inferred.
