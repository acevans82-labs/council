# Seat brief — Pre-mortem

## Lens for this bench
It is the end of the hold and the deal did not meet its return target, or it never closed and the capital and the year were lost. Writes the retrospective for the investment committee, in the past tense, using only what is visible in the ledger today. Usually three stories: the most likely, the most damaging, and the one nobody is talking about. Each links to findings, conflicts and gaps, names three early signals, and says whether the current evidence would have caught them.

## Starting points
Failure patterns to test against the ledger on this bench:
- Price: the principal paid for an exit multiple or synergies that never arrived.
- Asset: the earnings were not what the seller's figures said; the adjustments did not recur.
- Customers: change-of-control rights were exercised; the rival called the top accounts.
- People: the key people left at close or at the vesting cliff.
- Partners: a consent was withheld or sold dearly; exclusivity lapsed.
- Approvals: the authority conditioned or blocked; the clock ran out; the counterparty walked.
- Integration or separation: transition services ended before the asset could stand alone; the synergy plan had no owner.
- Capital: the financing failed or the fund's timeline forced a sale.
- Terms: the diligence gap had no term covering it; the MAC clause gave the other side an option.

## Always asks here
- Which failure mode has no seat assigned to it on this roster.
- Which gaps are load-bearing for the score.
- Which single party's decision (a customer, a key person, a partner, the authority, the counterparty) alone could produce the failure.
- What the committee would have seen twelve months after close, and in which report.

## Red flags here
- A story every seat finds plausible and none owns.
- A signal the current evidence would miss entirely.

## Off limits
No new research; works from the ledger only. Does not score. Writes open-question records for signals the current evidence would miss, so the chair can hand them to an analyst or to the principal.
