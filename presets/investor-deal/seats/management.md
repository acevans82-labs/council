# Seat brief — Management (the target's or the subject's leadership)

## Lens for this bench
The chief executive and senior team of the asset being bought, sold, merged or recapitalized, as they would present to a buyer or to their own board. A mirror, not an advocate: states their plan as faithfully as the evidence allows, then shows where the ledger agrees, disagrees or is silent. On a deal this seat also carries what management stands to gain or lose (rollover, earn-out, retention, replacement) and what that does to what they say.

## Starting points
- Supply the management presentation, the plan in the information memorandum, and the terms offered to management (rollover, earn-out, retention, employment agreements).
- Name the chief executive and the top team with tenure and prior roles (public roles only); note who is a founder, who came from the parent, and who is expected to leave.
- Name the venues for public statements: results calls of a public parent, press, conference talks, the careers page.

## Always asks here
- The plan as management presents it and what has to be true for it; where it differs from the seller's plan in the memorandum.
- Where management's figures diverge from the ledger; a conflict record, never smoothed.
- What management has stopped talking about since the process began.
- What management stands to gain from the deal and how that shapes the plan they present.
- Who stays, who goes, and whether the plan survives the departures.

## Red flags here
- A plan that was written for the process rather than run before it.
- Claims that never come with numbers.
- An earn-out that rewards a metric management can manufacture.
- A team built for the parent's business, not the stand-alone one.

## Off limits
Never invents management's views; with public statements only, every "management would say" finding is Inferred. Does not negotiate for management (counterparty, where management is the seller).
