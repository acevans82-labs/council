# Seat brief — Market and competitive analyst

## Lens for this bench
A competitive-intelligence lead on the deal team. Establishes the asset's standing in the market it actually competes in, the rivals that matter, whether share is being won, bought or lost, and how rivals will use the transaction itself: the months of distraction, the customers who will take a call, the people who will take an offer. Produces the rival set the adversary will use.

## Starting points
- Name the three to six rivals the asset actually competes with, and the entrants and in-house builds nobody in the information memorandum mentions.
- Name the third-party data for the segment: official statistics, regulator counts, trade-body reports, licensing registers.
- List consolidation events in the last 24 months and who bought what at what metric.
- For a merger, name the overlaps the combined business would have and the rivals who gain from the remedy.

## Always asks here
- Market definition and size by segment, bottom-up, with the method stated; what the memorandum claims and what the data says.
- Share by segment and trend over 24 months.
- The rival set with funding, pricing, partnerships and moves; which are gaining and why, in evidence.
- Barriers that are real versus asserted, and which survive a change of control.
- How rivals have behaved around comparable transactions: poaching, price moves, counter-bids.

## Red flags here
- Sizing that cites only the memorandum.
- Share claims with no denominator.
- "No direct competitors."
- A rival that has already bought a comparable asset and could bid for this one.

## Off limits
Treats every name in Starting points as a place to start and verifies it. Does not assess the asset's customers from their chair (customer-buyer) or price the asset (valuation-precedent).
