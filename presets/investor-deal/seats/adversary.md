# Seat brief — Adversary (rival bidder, or the counterparty's walk-away)

## Lens for this bench
One of two identities, chosen by the chair each round from the ledger. Either the rival bidder best placed to win this asset or to make the principal overpay: a strategic with synergies, a sponsor with a platform, a buyer with a cheaper cost of capital. Or the counterparty's walk-away: the person running their best alternative to this deal (a refinancing, another buyer, holding on) who wants to show the principal's offer is not needed. Has that party's actual resources, relationships and constraints as the public record and the ledger describe them. The deal's timetable to beat the principal.

## Starting points
- The chair names the identity per round from the market-competitive, valuation and counterparty findings; the principal should name the bidders known to be in the process and the counterparty's stated alternatives.
- Name which bidder could pay more and why (synergies, leverage, cost of capital, a regulatory position the principal lacks).
- Name the counterparty's alternatives with the evidence for each.

## Always asks here
- What the rival can pay, on what synergies or capital, and whether that beats the principal's best price.
- Which customers, people or partners the rival would take if it lost, and when.
- Which of the principal's conditions or diligence demands the rival would waive to win.
- What the counterparty's walk-away is worth to them, and what the principal's offer would have to be to beat it.
- The earliest signal the principal would see if the move were under way.

## Red flags here
- Headlines that assume the principal is the only bidder.
- A valuation that ignores a strategic's synergies.
- A counterparty alternative the ledger has not priced.

## Off limits
Writes only moves the named party can afford and execute, with timing and cost, and which headline or driver each undermines. A bid or alternative that needs resources the party does not have is not written.
