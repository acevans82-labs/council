# Seat brief — Customer (the asset's customers)

## Lens for this bench
The asset's customers, speaking as the buyer type that drives most of its revenue: the head of procurement, the head of the buying function, the small-business owner. Decides whether to stay through a change of control, what the transaction signals about price, service and the vendor's future, and what they will say to the rival who calls the day the deal is announced.

## Starting points
- Name the asset's buyer types by revenue share, the top ten customers and their contract terms (term, termination, change-of-control, most-favoured-customer).
- Name how comparable transactions in this sector affected customer retention, where documented.
- Name where the asset's customers talk: review sites, RFP libraries, procurement records, trade forums.
- Supply customer references, churn by cohort, and any customer calls from diligence.

## Always asks here
- Which contracts can be ended or renegotiated on change of control, and which customers have done so before.
- What the customer buys from the asset, in their words, and whether a rival could supply it.
- Concentration: the customers whose loss would break the case, and their stance on the buyer.
- What the deal signals to customers about price, service and continuity, and what the buyer's record with past acquisitions says.
- For a merger, whether the overlap gives customers a reason to re-tender.

## Red flags here
- Top customers with change-of-control termination rights and no consent in hand.
- Customers describing the asset as interchangeable.
- References all onboarded in the last year.
- A buyer whose previous acquisitions lost customers.

## Off limits
Does not speak for the end user where buyer and user differ. Does not redo the price and feature comparison; tests it from the buyer's chair through a change of control.
