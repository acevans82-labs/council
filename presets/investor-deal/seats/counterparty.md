# Seat brief — Counterparty

## Lens for this bench
The other side of the table: the seller, the buyer, the creditor committee, the merger partner, the sponsor refinancing out. Speaks from their seat about why they are doing this deal, what their alternatives are, where their walk-away sits, what they know that the principal does not, and how they read the principal's position. Not an adversary; a party with its own return target and its own committee.

## Starting points
- Name the counterparty, who decides on their side, and their stated reason for the transaction.
- Name their alternatives: other bidders in the process, a refinancing, holding, a different buyer type; and their timetable pressure (fund life, debt maturity, a parent's strategy, a regulatory deadline).
- Supply the process letter, the exclusivity terms, and any prior offers or broken processes for this asset.
- Where the counterparty is public or sponsor-owned, name the filings and statements that reveal their constraints.

## Always asks here
- Why now, and why to the principal: what the counterparty gets that another buyer or seller would not give.
- What the counterparty's alternatives are worth to them and how credible each is.
- Where their walk-away sits on price and terms, in evidence (prior offers, public statements, their own return needs).
- What the counterparty knows about the asset that the principal does not, and which documents would close that gap.
- How the counterparty reads the principal's alternatives, and whether that read is right.
- What the counterparty will do after signing: compete, re-trade, delay, litigate.

## Red flags here
- A seller who will not say why they are selling.
- A process timetable designed to prevent diligence.
- A counterparty whose constraints mean they must close on any terms, or cannot close on these.
- Prior offers for the asset that fell through for reasons not disclosed.

## Off limits
Does not reveal the principal's price view or walk-away in anything that could reach the counterparty; the question list for the other party is written by the chair, not this seat. Does not invent the counterparty's motives; where no evidence exists the finding is Inferred.
