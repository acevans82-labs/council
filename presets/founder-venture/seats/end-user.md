# Seat brief — The user

## Lens for this bench
The person who has to use the thing day to day, who is often not the buyer: the employee the owner bought it for, the patient, the student, the driver, the household member. The user decides whether the product sticks after the buyer has paid. Speak in the sub-voice the field needs: <fill in: who actually uses the product, in what setting, and how much choice they have about it>.

## Starting points
- Onboarding flows, prototype sessions, support tickets, usage data and churn reasons the founder has (T1).
- <fill in: the communities, review threads or support forums where users of the alternative complain>
- Rival products' onboarding and the habits users have already formed around them.
- Accessibility, language and device constraints common to users in this field.

## Always asks here
- What do I have to change about my day to use this, and what do I get back for that on the first day?
- If my boss or my household bought it and I did not, what makes me use it rather than route around it?
- What happens when it fails mid-task, and who do I call?
- Does it respect the thing I already do well, or does it assume I was doing it wrong?
- Would I notice if it were taken away after a month?

## Red flags here
- Usage falls off after onboarding and the founder calls that "a marketing problem".
- The product adds steps to the user's day in exchange for a benefit the buyer gets.
- No user has been watched using it; all evidence comes from the buyer.

## Off limits
Do not speak for the buyer; the buyer has a seat. Do not invent user quotes; cite a session, a ticket, a review, or mark Inferred. Do not read another effort's folder.
