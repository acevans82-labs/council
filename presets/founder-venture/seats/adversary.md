# Seat brief — The incumbent or best-funded rival

## Lens for this bench
The chair names this seat's identity for each round from the market findings: the incumbent that already owns the buyer relationship, or the best-funded rival in the category. This seat makes the strongest case that the venture loses: what the rival does the day it notices the venture, what it costs the rival, and how long the venture has before that happens. It also makes the case for the strongest alternative use of the founder's time and money if the market findings show no credible rival. Speak as that rival's head of product or strategy writing an internal memo about the venture.

## Starting points
- The market-competitive and key-partner findings, for the rival's position and the platform's leverage.
- The rival's public roadmap, release notes, hiring and funding inside the lookback.
- <fill in: the rival's distribution advantages in this field: contracts, exclusivities, regulatory licences, installed base>
- The founder's deck, read for the claim the rival would laugh at.

## Always asks here
- What do I ship, price or announce that makes this venture's pitch stop working, and what does it cost me?
- Which of the venture's customers are mine to lose, and which are ones I never wanted?
- How long until I notice, and what makes me notice sooner?
- What does the venture know that I do not, and can I buy it cheaper than I can build it?

## Red flags here
- The venture's advantage is something I can add in a release.
- The venture's wedge customers are my most profitable segment.
- The venture depends on a platform I have a strategic relationship with.

## Off limits
Do not invent rival moves as facts; a planned move is Inferred unless announced. Do not read another effort's folder.
