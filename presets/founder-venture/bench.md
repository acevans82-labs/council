# Bench — Founder vetting a venture

## The principal's seat
The principal is a founder or entrepreneur deciding whether to commit time and money to an idea, a venture or a pivot. In this hat the principal can decide alone whether to proceed, what to build first, how much to spend before the next checkpoint and whom to bring in. The principal cannot decide what customers will pay, what rivals will do, what a funder will back or what a co-founder or spouse will accept. The council addresses the principal in the third person as "the principal" or "the founder", never "you". The principal is both the person asking and the person proposing, so the council's job is to test the pitch rather than to flatter it.

## What this bench assesses
An idea: a venture, a product line, a pivot, a side business before it becomes the main business. Typical efforts are one idea each, with whatever the founder has (a deck, a landing page, a prototype, interview notes, a spreadsheet). Examples: a subscription service for a trade that still buys by phone; pivoting an existing tool from consumers to small firms after consumer growth stalled.

## The question the bench answers
score_label: Probability this venture reaches its next milestone on the evidence

The milestone is named at intake (first paying customers, a repeatable sales motion, a seed round, break-even, a regulatory clearance). If the principal names none, the council assumes "ten paying customers who renew" and says so.

### Drivers
| key | driver | weight | 9–10 | 5–6 | 0–2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| problem_demand | The problem and the demand to fix it | 20 | The problem is frequent, costly and named unprompted by the people who have it; there is evidence of pull (waitlists, paid pilots, workarounds people already pay for) | The problem is real but occasional; interest expressed, little paid for; demand inferred from analogues rather than shown | The problem is asserted by the founder only; no one has been found who would pay; a solution looking for a problem |
| solution_differentiation | The solution and what keeps it different | 20 | Solves the problem in a way rivals cannot copy within the horizon (data, distribution, licence, cost structure); users prefer it in a test against the alternative | A better version of what exists; the advantage is real but copyable in a year; preference not yet tested | A feature-level difference an incumbent could ship in a quarter; no evidence anyone prefers it |
| market_timing | Market size and timing | 15 | A documented shift (a rule, a technology, a cost curve, a behavior) opened the window inside the lookback and it is still open; the reachable market supports the milestone | The market exists and grows; no clear reason for now rather than three years ago; size estimates rest on top-down figures | The window has passed or has not opened; the reachable market is too small for the milestone or shrinking |
| economics | Unit economics | 15 | Shown on real transactions: contribution positive now or at a scale inside the milestone; payback inside a known window; price tested | Modeled on reasonable assumptions, not yet shown; positive contribution depends on one or two levers that have not moved yet | No unit model, or one that needs a scale the milestone does not reach; price never put to a buyer |
| team_execution | The team and its ability to execute | 15 | The team has built and sold in this domain before; the skills the milestone needs are on the team or hired; the founder's time is committed | Strong on one side (build or sell) with the other missing and a credible plan to fill it | No relevant record; a key skill absent with no plan; the founder is part-time with no date to change that |
| capital_risk | Capital to the milestone and the risk of running out | 15 | Capital to the milestone is in hand or committed with margin, and the milestone is one a next funder recognises | Capital covers most of the way; a raise is needed mid-path and there is precedent for it at this stage | No funded runway; the milestone needs more capital than ventures of this kind raise at this stage; one funding source and no fallback |

### Standards
Defaults per skills/scoring. In addition: a score above 70 requires problem_demand at 7 or higher on Corroborated evidence, since nothing else on this bench matters if nobody wants the thing.

## Assessment outline
Subject sections, in order: problem and demand; solution and differentiation; market and timing; economics and capital; the team and what it must build.

Stakeholder order: management (the pitch as the founder would make it), customer-buyer, end-user, channel-partner, key-partner, investor, employee, co-decider, legal-counsel.

## Primary sources in this field
T1 is what the founder supplies: the deck, the model, interview notes, pilot data, letters of intent, the cap table. T2 in this field: company and charity registers; patent and trademark offices; the primary regulator's registers and guidance; official statistics on the market's size and the buyers' numbers; peer-reviewed or official studies of the problem; procurement and tender records where the buyer is public; standards bodies. T3: funding databases, press, review sites, app stores, job postings, rival websites and pricing pages, founder forums. The seat briefs name where each seat starts; the principal should replace the fill-in prompts with the registers and rivals of the actual field.

## The other party
None by default. The founder is both principal and proponent. The `management` seat mirrors the founder's pitch as the council heard it at intake, so that the customers, partners, investor and red team have a concrete proposition to react to; its findings are the pitch's claims, tagged and graded like any other, not the council's view. If the founder wants a briefing for a co-founder, an investor or an adviser, set `other_party` to that reader and the chair produces the question list and briefing per skills/briefing.

## Confidentiality
The founder's deck, model, pilot data and customer names stay in the effort folder and are never quoted into another effort. The council does not contact customers, partners or rivals; it reads what is public and what the founder supplies. Keep documents uncommitted by uncommenting the `documents/` line in `.gitignore` if the repository is shared.
