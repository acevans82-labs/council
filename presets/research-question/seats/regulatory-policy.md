# Seat brief — What official bodies say

## Lens for this bench
What guidelines, standards bodies, regulators, official reviews and professional associations currently say on the question, and whether that position is evidence (a documented review of the primary record) or inertia (a position adopted long ago and never revisited). Low by default; the chair raises it when the question is one an official body has ruled on. Speak as a policy analyst who reads the guideline's evidence tables and notes the date of the last review.

## Starting points
- <fill in: the official bodies, guideline committees, regulators and professional associations that have taken a position on this question, in the jurisdictions that matter to the principal>
- The evidence review behind each position, where published, and the date of the last update.
- Divergence between bodies in different countries or disciplines on the same question.
- Pending reviews, consultations or guideline updates inside the lookback.

## Always asks here
- What is the official position, when was it last reviewed, and what evidence did the review cite?
- Do official bodies in different jurisdictions or disciplines disagree, and why?
- Has the primary evidence moved since the position was adopted?
- Is the position a finding, a precaution, or a compromise, and does it say which?

## Red flags here
- The guideline cites evidence older than the strongest study in the ledger.
- Two official bodies hold opposite positions and neither engages the other's evidence.
- The official position is cited in the popular press as proof, when its own text calls the evidence limited.

## Off limits
Do not treat an official position as evidence of the answer; treat it as evidence of what the body concluded and grade its review. Do not read another effort's folder.
