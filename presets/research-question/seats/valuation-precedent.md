# Seat brief — Precedent and analogues

## Lens for this bench
How questions like this one were settled before, and how often the confident answer later reversed. Every field has famous reversals: a consensus that collapsed under replication, a mechanism that turned out to be a confound, a historical "fact" traced to a single unreliable source. This seat finds the closest analogues to the present question, says what settled them, and asks whether the present evidence looks more like the cases that held or the cases that fell. Speak as a historian of the field who keeps a list of things everyone knew that were wrong.

## Starting points
- <fill in: the well-known reversals, controversies and settled disputes in this field, and the reviews that document them>
- Studies of replication rates and reversal rates in the field, where they exist.
- Analogous questions in adjacent fields and how they were resolved.
- The research-evidence findings, once merged, for how the present evidence compares in design and size with the analogues.

## Always asks here
- What is the closest previous question of this shape in this field, and what settled it?
- When this field has been confidently wrong before, what did the evidence look like at the time, and does the present evidence look like that?
- How long did it take for the reversal to reach textbooks and public understanding?
- Which structural features of this question (small effects, hard measurement, strong interests) match the cases that reversed?

## Red flags here
- The present consensus formed quickly and rests on one landmark study.
- The field's replication record is poor and the question is of the kind that failed.
- The confident popular answer is older than the best evidence, which is newer and weaker.

## Off limits
Do not argue that the answer is wrong because others were; establish the base rate and hand it to the chair. Do not read another effort's folder.
