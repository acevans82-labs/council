# Bench — Board director, US healthcare services and benefits

## The principal's seat
The principal is a director, board chair or senior operator assessing a US healthcare-services or benefits company they govern, run or are considering joining. In this hat the principal can put items on the board agenda, ask management for information, vote on the matters reserved to the board (strategy, budget, capital, leadership, major transactions, regulatory posture) and decide whether to join or stay. The principal cannot run the company day to day, cannot direct staff below the chief executive, and cannot act alone on matters reserved to the full board, to shareholders, or to a parent. The council addresses the principal in the third person, as "the principal", never "you"; every output should be forwardable to the rest of the board unedited.

## What this bench assesses
A company in one of five business types, each with its own program rules, buyers and channel: (1) benefits administration and defined-contribution platforms, including ICHRA administrators and the infrastructure beneath them; (2) Medicare and health-plan distribution: agencies, call centers, online brokers, lead generators; (3) managed behavioral health and carve-outs, including Medicaid behavioral contracts and EAP; (4) supplemental benefits and wellness vendors selling to Medicare Advantage plans, Medicare Supplement carriers and employers; (5) managed care services: TPAs, care management, utilization management, network services. Say which type the effort is at intake; several seats change their lens by type. Two examples: an ICHRA administrator the principal chairs, ahead of a raise; a Medicare distribution business the principal has been asked to join as an independent director after the marketing and compensation rule changes.

## Primary sources in this field
Treat these as T2. Each persona checks the group for its seat and the "any company" group first; extend `skills/exhaustive-retrieval/references/regulatory-sources.md` when a new business type is added.

**Any company.** SEC EDGAR (public parents, rivals, carriers; Form D for private raises). State Secretary of State registrations (entity status, officers, foreign qualification). PACER and state court dockets; state attorney-general actions. USPTO trademarks (brand conflicts). FTC and DOJ actions; HSR notices. HHS OCR breach portal and state breach notifications. OFAC lists; the OIG exclusion list (LEIE) for owners and officers.

**Benefits administration, ICHRA and defined contribution.** IRS, DOL and HHS ICHRA rules and guidance; Treasury and DOL notices; EBSA enforcement. Federal Register and congressional trackers for ICHRA codification and expansion. CMS/CCIIO Marketplace rules, agent and broker conduct rules, enrollment and subsidy data; state-based exchange reports. State DOI producer and TPA licensing lookups; NIPR; state bulletins on ICHRA and individual-market conditions. State money-transmitter and premium-payment rules where premium flows are handled. NAIC individual-market reports; carrier rate filings via SERFF. Industry data-standards bodies for benefits enrollment and ICHRA; benefits-consultant surveys and the KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey.

**Medicare Advantage supplemental benefits and wellness.** CMS Part C and D final rules; the annual rate announcement; HPMS memos on supplemental benefits, uniformity, SSBCI and marketing. CMS plan benefit package (PBP) public-use files; MA enrollment by plan and county; Star Ratings. Medicare Plan Finder benefit data (prevalence of a benefit type by plan). State DOI Medicare Supplement rate filings (SERFF) and regulations. Actuarial and policy-research reports on supplemental benefits. Employer wellness rules (EEOC, HIPAA nondiscrimination, ADA) and DOL guidance.

**Managed behavioral health and carve-outs.** State Medicaid agency procurement portals, contract awards, MCO and BHO contracts, RFP libraries. The CMS Medicaid managed-care rule (network adequacy, MLR, encounter data, parity); CMS Medicaid enrollment and financing data; State Medicaid Director letters. MHPAEA comparative-analysis enforcement reports (DOL, HHS, state DOIs). SAMHSA (42 CFR Part 2, block grants, 988 and crisis policy); state behavioral-health authorities. State DOI licensing for utilization-review agents and MBHOs; NCQA and URAC accreditation status. Court dockets and state AG actions involving carve-outs; parity litigation.

**Medicare and health-plan distribution.** CMS Medicare Communications and Marketing Guidelines; Part C and D marketing rules; TPMO requirements; agent and broker compensation limits and annual notices. CMS complaint tracking (CTM) data where public; CMS enforcement and civil money penalties; OIG reports on MA marketing. State DOI producer licensing and appointment lookups; state telemarketing, call-recording and consent laws; state DOI complaint indices. FCC and FTC rules on TCPA, lead generation and consent; court dockets for TCPA class actions in the channel. Public distribution comparables' filings and calls for channel economics and persistency. Carrier public statements on distribution strategy and captive channels.

**Payers and managed care (context for the carrier and CMS seats).** State DOI rate filings (SERFF), financial and market-conduct exams, enforcement. NAIC statutory statements. CMS MA Star Ratings, enrollment, bid and RADV data. Healthcare.gov and state-exchange QHP certifications; MLR reports. Carrier earnings calls and investor days for line-of-business commentary.

**Health systems and providers (provider seat).** CMS Care Compare, cost reports (HCRIS), PECOS and NPPES. State discharge data, network-adequacy filings and wait-time reports where published. Provider-association positions (AHA, AMA, state medical societies, behavioral-health provider associations). IRS Form 990 for not-for-profit systems.

**Labor (labor union seat).** DOL Form 5500 filings for Taft-Hartley health and welfare funds. Union and federation policy statements and lobbying disclosures (LDA filings). The NLRB case database for organizing activity. Public-sector retiree health-plan procurements.

## The other party
The company's management team. They may receive the question list and the briefing deck per `skills/briefing`: the method, the score, the insights, the data requests, the questions and next steps. They may not see driver rationales, individual findings, stakeholder views, the red team's work, or anything the principal supplied under board confidentiality. Where a parent or sponsor sits on the board, decide at intake whether they count as "the other party" or as the principal's side.

## Confidentiality
Board packs, management accounts, carrier and state contracts, and anything else the principal supplies under board confidentiality go in the effort's `documents/` folder as T1 and are never quoted into another effort's ledger. Protected health information must never be supplied; if a document contains it, the principal redacts before supplying. Decide at setup whether `documents/` is committed or kept local; the default for board material is local.
