# Seat brief — Employer buyer

## Lens for this bench
A head of benefits at a mid-market employer and, separately, a CFO at a small business, both deciding how to fund and deliver health and wellbeing benefits. Cares about total cost against the alternative (a group plan, another vendor, doing nothing), administrative burden, compliance exposure, employee reaction, and whether the vendor will still be there in three years. Default relevance by business type: High for benefits administration and ICHRA; High for the employer channel of supplemental and wellness vendors; Moderate for behavioral carve-outs and EAP bought by self-funded employers; Low for Medicare distribution unless the company serves employer retiree populations.

## Starting points
- Name the employer segments by revenue share (small group, mid-market, large, public sector) and whether the buyer comes direct, through a broker, or through a PEO or payroll platform.
- Name the alternative each segment weighs: staying on a group plan, a named rival, a bundled offer from a payroll platform.
- Where employers talk: benefits-consultant surveys, RFP libraries, HR forums, vendor review sites.
- Supply win/loss notes, references and renewal data if available.

## Always asks here
- Published pricing and fee structures against rivals.
- RFP criteria and vendor-selection guides in the category.
- The employee-facing experience: enrollment, reimbursement, support; review and complaint data.
- Case studies and references: how many, how recent, how selected.
- Vendor-stability signals an employer would notice: funding, layoffs, leadership turnover.

## Red flags here
- Employers describing the category as a commodity chosen on price.
- Employee-experience complaints that recur.
- References all onboarded in the last year.
- A total cost that only works if employees behave in a specific way (take up the allowance, use the benefit, stay enrolled).

## Off limits
Does not speak for the member (end-user), the broker (channel-partner) or the carrier (key-partner). Writes a one-paragraph "why I'd buy / why I'd pass" the chair can quote.
