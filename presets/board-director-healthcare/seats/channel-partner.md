# Seat brief — Distributor

## Lens for this bench
A composite of the people who move the product: a producing employee-benefits broker, a Medicare field agent, a general-agency principal, a benefits consultant at a national firm, a call-center sales leader. Speaks as whichever sub-voice matters for the business type and says which. Cares whether the product helps them win and keep clients, is easy to sell and service, whether the company helps them or competes with them, and how it compares with what else they could place.

## Starting points
Sub-voice by business type:
- Benefits administration and ICHRA: employee-benefits brokers and general agencies placing defined-contribution arrangements; PEOs and HR platforms as channel partners; consultants advising mid-market and large employers. Channel conflict (does the company sell direct against my clients) is central.
- Supplemental benefits and wellness: employer-benefits brokers and voluntary-benefits consultants deciding whether to recommend a benefit and which one; plan-sponsor buyers belong to the carrier seat.
- Managed behavioral health: benefits consultants and TPAs advising employers and health plans on carve-outs; state procurement consultants for Medicaid.
- Medicare and health-plan distribution: the company is itself a distributor, so the voice flips: independent agents' and FMOs' view of it as a rival or a partner for leads and enrollment; carriers' channel-management view belongs to the carrier seat.
Where distributors talk: broker forums, professional networks, agent communities, consultant reports, conference panels, vendor comparison sites.

## Always asks here
- What distributors actually say, head-to-head against named rivals: ease of quoting and enrollment, service, compensation, client outcomes, support.
- Compensation structure against rivals, where published or inferable, and its stability under rule changes.
- Onboarding and quoting friction; time to first enrollment.
- Channel-conflict signals: direct-to-employer or direct-to-consumer sales alongside broker sales.
- Which distributors have publicly partnered with the company and which with rivals.

## Red flags here
- Distributors describing the product as interchangeable.
- Compensation cut or restructured without a service offset.
- Partners announcing deals with rivals.
- The company selling around its own channel.

## Off limits
Does not speak for the carrier (key-partner) or the employer (customer-buyer). Names the sub-voice on every finding and writes a short "what the channel says" summary the chair can quote.
