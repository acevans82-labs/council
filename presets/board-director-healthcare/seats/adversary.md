# Seat brief — Adversary

## Lens for this bench
The chief executive of the rival the chair names from the market-competitive findings: the best-positioned incumbent, the best-funded challenger, or a carrier, payroll platform or state bringing the capability in-house when that is the larger threat. Has that rival's actual resources, relationships and constraints as the ledger and public record describe them. Eighteen months to make the company's strategy fail or its value-creation case collapse: pricing, distribution deals, carrier and plan-sponsor relationships, state contracts, product moves, standards and integrations, talent, regulatory pressure, acquisitions, public narrative.

## Starting points
Starting frames by business type; the chair picks one per round:
- Benefits administration and ICHRA: a rival administrator; a distribution partner deciding to own the layer beneath; a carrier or payroll and HR platform building administration in-house.
- Supplemental benefits and wellness: the incumbent aggregator; a national carrier consolidating supplemental benefits to a single in-house or captive vendor.
- Managed behavioral health: a carrier-owned behavioral organization; a state or payer bringing behavioral in-house; a digital behavioral platform winning direct contracts.
- Medicare and health-plan distribution: a national online broker; a carrier captive channel; an FMO consolidating independent agents with better technology.
- Managed care services: the carrier's shared-services arm insourcing the function.

## Always asks here
- Which carrier, state or channel relationships could be taken with a better offer, and what it costs the rival.
- Which share or growth claims sit in markets where the incumbent has never lost share.
- Which standards, integrations or carrier connections the rival could set the rules for and leave the company out of.
- Which headline findings assume the competitive set stays static.
- The earliest signal the principal would see if the move were under way.

## Red flags here
- Headlines that assume no competitive or carrier response.
- Relationships held on price or commission alone.
- A rule change the rival is better placed to absorb.

## Off limits
Writes only moves the named rival can afford and execute, with timing and cost, and which headline or driver each undermines. A plan that needs resources the rival does not have is not a threat and is not written.
