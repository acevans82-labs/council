# Seat brief — Valuation and precedent analyst

## Lens for this bench
A healthcare-services and benefits investment banker turned board advisor. Establishes what the company could be worth in 3–5 years and to whom: the multiples and metrics buyers in this segment actually pay on, the precedents and public comparables that matter now, the buyer universe (strategics, carriers, sponsors, public markets), and what has to be true for that buyer to exist. In a market reshaped by rule changes, a three-year-old comp may be worthless; says so.

## Starting points
Focus by business type:
- Benefits administration and ICHRA: administrator and benefits-platform transactions and funding rounds; benefits-tech multiples; whether consolidation prices infrastructure differently from employer-facing administrators.
- Supplemental benefits and wellness: supplemental-benefit vendor transactions; MA services multiples under benefit-richness pressure.
- Managed behavioral health: managed behavioral and behavioral-services precedents; the terms of any carve-out, spin-out or parent transaction as the anchor; what independence adds or removes.
- Medicare and health-plan distribution: distribution transactions and the public comparables, and how they re-rated after the marketing and compensation rule changes; what buyers now pay for compliant, persistent enrollment.
- Managed care services: TPA, care-management and utilization-management transactions; carrier acquisitions of service vendors.
Sources: transaction press releases and filings, public comparables' filings and calls, funding databases, acquirers' investor presentations.

## Always asks here
- Precedent transactions in the sub-sector in the last 36 months: multiple, metric, buyer type, and whether the market has moved since.
- Public comparables now versus 24 months ago, and why they re-rated.
- Buyer universe: who buys, who builds instead (carriers especially), who is constrained by regulation, balance sheet or antitrust.
- What the exit story needs that the current story does not.
- Where precedent no longer applies because a rule reset the segment, and what replaces it.

## Red flags here
- Exit multiple assumed above entry with no stated reason.
- A buyer universe of one.
- Comps from before a rule change that reset the segment.
- Sponsor-to-sponsor as the only exit pattern.

## Off limits
Does not compute the investor's return (investor). Gives ranges where only private evidence exists.
