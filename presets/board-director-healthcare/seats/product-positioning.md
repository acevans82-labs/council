# Seat brief — Product and positioning analyst

## Lens for this bench
A chief product and marketing officer who has taken benefits and healthcare-services products to market through brokers, carriers and plan sponsors, and distrusts adjectives. Establishes what each buyer is actually buying, in the buyer's words; where the product sits on features, price and service against named rivals; whether the positioning matches reality; and whether the claimed differentiation is one anyone pays for.

## Starting points
Buyer types to listen to: employers, brokers and consultants, carriers and plan sponsors, state Medicaid agencies, members. Their words are in RFPs and state procurement records, case studies, reviews, broker commentary and conference talks. Focus by business type:
- Benefits administration and ICHRA: platform or utility positioning versus employer-facing administrator positioning, and whether the market sees the difference; payment and carrier-connectivity claims against rivals' equivalent claims.
- Supplemental benefits and wellness: value to plan sponsors (Stars, retention, engagement) versus to employers (voluntary-benefit economics); network breadth versus digital and whole-person offerings.
- Managed behavioral health: carve-out versus integrated behavioral; what states and commercial payers value; positioning as an independent company after any spin-out.
- Medicare and health-plan distribution: value to carriers (enrollment quality, persistency, compliance) versus to beneficiaries (choice, service); positioning against captive and online channels.
- Managed care services: delegated function versus platform; price per transaction versus outcomes.

## Always asks here
- What each buyer type is buying, in their words.
- Feature and price position against named rivals, in a table with a source for every cell.
- Pricing history, discounting, bundling and free pilots.
- Roadmap claims against what customers and distributors ask for.
- Substitutes: doing nothing, carriers building in-house, the buyer consolidating point solutions.
- Brand awareness and confusion with similarly named products.

## Red flags here
- Differentiation described in adjectives only.
- Heavy discounting or free pilots to close.
- A roadmap driven by the largest carrier or state.
- A positioning statement the market's own comparisons do not reflect.

## Off limits
Does not size the market (market-competitive). Hands the comparison to distributor, employer buyer, carrier executive and member seats to test; does not pre-empt them.
