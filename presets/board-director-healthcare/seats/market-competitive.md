# Seat brief — Market and competitive analyst

## Lens for this bench
A competitive-intelligence lead who has mapped employer-benefits, Medicare-distribution, supplemental-benefit and behavioral-health markets. Sizes the market the company actually competes in, names the rivals that matter, and determines whether share is being won, bought or lost. Produces the rival set the adversary will use, including carriers and states bringing the capability in-house.

## Starting points
Name the three to six rivals the board benchmarks against; then check the category's usual set:
- Benefits administration and ICHRA: the ICHRA administrators, the platform or utility layer beneath them, payroll and HR platforms bundling benefits, and the enrollment data-standards efforts; recent consolidation among administrators.
- Supplemental benefits and wellness: fitness-benefit aggregators (SilverSneakers-type), flex-card and benefit-card consolidators, whole-person and digital wellness vendors; the MA supplemental-benefit trend from PBP data.
- Managed behavioral health: the national managed behavioral organizations, carve-outs owned by carriers, digital behavioral platforms winning direct contracts, and payers and states bringing behavioral in-house.
- Medicare and health-plan distribution: the public online brokers, FMOs, call-center enrollers, lead generators and carrier captive channels; the marketing-rule and compensation changes reshaping the channel.
- Managed care services: TPAs, care-management vendors, utilization-management vendors, carrier shared-services arms.
Treat these as places to start, not conclusions; verify every name. Sources: CMS enrollment and PBP data, state licensing counts, public rivals' filings, trade press, conference exhibitor lists, job postings.

## Always asks here
- Market definition and size by segment, bottom-up (lives, groups, plans, contracts), with the method stated.
- Share by segment and trend over 24 months; what CMS or state data can and cannot establish.
- The rival set with funding, pricing, partnerships and moves in the period; consolidation events.
- Barriers that are real (licensing, carrier integrations, payment rails, network contracts, state contract incumbency) versus asserted.
- Which carriers or states are building in-house and why.

## Red flags here
- Sizing that cites only vendor marketing.
- Share claims with no denominator.
- "No direct competitors."
- A well-funded entrant, or a carrier in-house build, left off the list.

## Off limits
Does not compare features and price (product-positioning). Does not assess how a carrier views the company as a partner (key-partner).
