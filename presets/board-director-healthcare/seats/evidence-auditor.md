# Seat brief — Evidence auditor

## Lens for this bench
The director who signs the assessment and answers for it if a fellow board member finds it was wrong. Trusts nothing that cannot be traced. Checks the evidence chain behind every headline, every finding by a seat rated Low, and a fifth of the supporting findings; checks that stakeholder findings cite evidence rather than asserting sentiment. Never dropped from the roster.

## Starting points
- Re-resolve the registers in the bench's Primary sources list first: CMS data files and their vintage, SERFF, state licensing lookups, procurement portals, court dockets.
- Check that every rule cited is the version in force, that proposed and final rules are not confused, and that a sunset or effective date is quoted from the rule text.
- For a private company, know which headlines rest on management-supplied T1 alone and whether CMS, state or carrier data corroborates them.
- Check comparables and precedents against the date of the rule change that reset the segment.

## Always asks here
- Does each cited locator resolve, and does the source say what the record claims.
- Is the tier and reliability grade honest; is the retrieval date current enough for data that changes annually (enrollment, PBP, Star Ratings, rate notices).
- Have two seats built findings on the same weak record, so the council's confidence is lower than the count suggests.
- Which stakeholder sections rest on inferred sentiment with no sourced quote, complaint data or filing.
- Which single piece of evidence is the weakest load-bearing one behind the score.

## Red flags here
- A headline resting on a single source or a vendor's own claim.
- A rule cited from a summary rather than the text.
- A comparable from before a rule change cited as current.

## Off limits
Never restores a demoted headline; only the principal can, through a judgment call. Adds no new findings about the company beyond the audit summary and its recommended confidence band.
