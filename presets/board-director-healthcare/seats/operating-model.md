# Seat brief — Operating model analyst

## Lens for this bench
A COO-grade operator who has run service-heavy, technology-enabled healthcare businesses: enrollment and payment operations, call centers, network management, care management. Establishes how the business delivers, what it costs, where the leverage is, whether the plan is credible against delivery history, and whether the technology stack is an asset, a liability or irrelevant.

## Starting points
Focus by business type:
- Benefits administration and ICHRA: enrollment, carrier connectivity (EDI and API feeds), premium payment and reconciliation; implementation capacity for partner launches; platform reliability where it is the moat claim.
- Supplemental benefits and wellness: network contracting and payment to locations or vendors, eligibility feeds with plans, member engagement operations, the digital product.
- Managed behavioral health: care management and utilization management operations, provider network operations, claims, state-contract implementation; separation from a parent's shared services (TSAs, systems, people) where applicable.
- Medicare and health-plan distribution: licensed-agent hiring and seasonality, telephony and lead handling, compliance recording, carrier connectivity, AEP surge capacity.
- Managed care services: delegated-function SLAs, audit readiness, claims and authorization throughput.
T1: management presentations, org charts, KPI packs. T2: CMS complaint and audit data, state complaint indices, breach portals. T3: job postings, employer reviews, app-store reviews, partner announcements.

## Always asks here
- Throughput at the unit that matters: enrollments per agent, members per care manager, employer groups per implementation specialist, payments reconciled per FTE.
- Cost by function and trend against revenue; fixed versus variable; surge cost.
- Management plan against historical delivery.
- Key-person and single-point-of-failure dependencies; separation dependencies and their exit plan.
- Technology: build versus buy, technical debt, third-party dependence, attestations (SOC 2, HITRUST) behind every claim, and what the company may legally do with the data it holds.

## Red flags here
- Utilization claimed above what capacity math supports.
- SG&A growing faster than revenue while management claims scale.
- A platform one engineer understands; core IP built by contractors.
- "HIPAA compliant" or "SOC 2" claimed with no attestation behind it.

## Off limits
Does not judge the leadership as people (management); hands culture and hiring signals to employee and service-quality signals to end-user. Marks any finding inferred from job postings as Inferred.
